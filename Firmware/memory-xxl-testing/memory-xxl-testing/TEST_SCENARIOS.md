## January 9, 2025

## 🎯 Testing Goals

1. **Usability**: Can users understand and play without help?
2. **Engagement**: Do users enjoy it and want to play again?
3. **Difficulty**: Is the game too easy, too hard, or just right?
4. **Social**: Does it work well in groups?
5. **Technical**: Do the hardware/software work reliably?

---

## 👥 Test Scenarios

### Scenario 1: First-Time Player (5 min)

**Persona**: 12-14 year old, never seen the game before

**Setup**:
- Game ready, tiles visible
- No explanation given initially

**Script**:
1. "Hi! This is a new game we're testing. Want to try it?"
2. [Show them the tiles and screen]
3. "Can you figure out what to do?" [DON'T HELP YET]
4. [Observe: do they press start? Do they understand?]
5. [After 30 sec, if stuck]: "Press the start button and watch what happens"
6. [Let them play one complete round]

**Observe**:
- [ ] Do they press start without prompting?
- [ ] Do they watch the pattern carefully?
- [ ] Do they understand they need to repeat it?
- [ ] Do they step on tiles correctly?
- [ ] Do they look at screen for feedback?
- [ ] Do they understand the score?

**Questions After**:
- "What did you think the game was about before starting?"
- "Was it clear what you needed to do?"
- "Was the pattern easy to remember?"
- "Would you play again?"

### Scenario 2: Competitive Player (5 min)

**Persona**: 15-17 year old, likes challenges

**Setup**:
- Let them play 2-3 rounds
- Mention there's a high score

**Script**:
1. "Want to try to beat the high score?" [Show current high score]
2. [Let them play multiple rounds]
3. [Observe their strategy and engagement]

**Observe**:
- [ ] Do they focus more after mentioning high score?
- [ ] Do they get frustrated or motivated by difficulty?
- [ ] Do they want to keep playing to improve?
- [ ] Do they ask about the scoring system?
- [ ] Do they develop a strategy?

**Questions After**:
- "Did the scoring make sense to you?"
- "Was the difficulty progression good?"
- "What would make you want to play more?"
- "Would you compete with friends?"

### Scenario 3: Group Play (7 min)

**Persona**: 3-4 friends (13-16 years old)

**Setup**:
- Multiple players taking turns

**Script**:

**Observe**:

- [ ] Do they cheer/react to each other's plays?
- [ ] Do they help each other strategize?

- [ ] Do they keep playing after first round?
- [ ] Do they compare scores?

**Questions After**:

## 📋 General Observation Checklist

For EVERY test session, note:

### Technical Performance:
- [ ] Did all tiles respond correctly?
- [ ] Were LEDs visible/clear?
- [ ] Did the screen update properly?
- [ ] Were there any glitches/bugs?
- [ ] Response time acceptable?

### User Behavior:
- [ ] Did they understand without help?
- [ ] Did they look engaged/bored?
- [ ] Did they make mistakes (what kind)?
- [ ] Did they want to play again?

## 🎤 Standard Questions (Ask Everyone)

### Before Playing:
1. "Have you seen anything like this before?"
2. "What do you think you'll need to do?"

### After Playing:
1. "On a scale of 1-10, how fun was it?"
2. "Would you play this again?"
3. "What was the best part?"
4. "What was the worst/most confusing part?"
5. "How would you explain this game to a friend?"

### Specific Follow-ups:
6. "Was the pattern display clear enough?"
7. "Did you understand your score?"
8. "Was it too easy, too hard, or just right?"
9. "Would you change anything?"
10. "Who would you play this with?"

## 💡 Tips for Testing

### Do:
✅ Be friendly and encouraging
✅ Let them fail and learn
✅ Take detailed notes
✅ Ask "why" to understand reasoning
✅ Film reactions (with permission)
✅ Thank everyone for participating

### Don't:
❌ Jump in too quickly to help
❌ Defend design choices during testing
❌ Lead them to specific answers
❌ Interrupt during gameplay
❌ Forget to note technical issues
